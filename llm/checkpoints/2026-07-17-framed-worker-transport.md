## Completed transport slice

`Function.runPreparedActivityAttempt` now runs the fixed private activity
wrapper through `worker.runActivityAttemptInSandbox`, rather than the legacy
aggregate `exec`/result path. The legacy `exec`/RPC transport remains only for
legacy `Function.run` and actor-system behavior.

For every physical Activity Protocol attempt:

- A fresh sandbox and one-shot worker are created, even for identical bundle
  bytes. Forced termination therefore cannot kill a concurrent attempt.
- The host validates and retains `start`, accepts `cancel`, owns the
  transcript validator, acknowledges each accepted component frame, and builds
  evidence locally. No bundle-supplied aggregate evidence is trusted.
- The host and runner use a dedicated `MessageChannel`. Runner lifecycle
  messages carry a random per-attempt authenticator generated by the host.
  The runner captures the port's send/listen methods and installs its listener
  before bundle evaluation; it retains only closure-backed authenticated send
  capability afterward. Host receipt is bound to the exact attempt and rejects
  unauthenticated port traffic.
- This prevents ordinary same-isolate JavaScript from forging protocol messages
  through `parentPort`, discovered active `MessagePort` handles, mutable
  `MessagePort.prototype`, or a patched `Map.prototype`. It is an integrity
  boundary for trusted Wharfie application code, **not** a hostile-code
  sandbox. A future adversarial-bundle mode needs a broker in a separate
  process/OS isolation boundary.
- External cancellation and deadlines have a bounded grace and terminate only
  their one-shot worker if it remains uncooperative. A valid terminal must be
  followed by runner closure; late authenticated frames and a hanging wrapper
  become failure rather than fabricated evidence. A finite ready watchdog also
  terminates a bundle that blocks before its wrapper reports readiness.
- Deadline precedence is explicit: cancellation accepted before the deadline
  owns its grace and may end as `cancelled` after the clock crosses the
  deadline. At/after the deadline, a new cancel does not displace the
  deadline-owned attempt. Early `deadline-exceeded` frames, post-deadline
  non-deadline frames without prior cancellation, and incompatible terminals
  after accepted cancellation fail closed.
- The framed worker path exposes no generic resource RPC or managed effects.
  It accepts `start`, `cancel`, ordered logs, and one terminal only.

## Important lifecycle boundary

The physical worker attempt begins after frozen external-bundle materialization
and sandbox preparation. Cancellation/deadline and ready-watchdog timers are
then attached before the runner is opened, so bundle evaluation and handler
execution are bounded.
An already-cancelled request prevents handler entry once the runner reports
ready, but it cannot suppress bundle top-level initialization, which is needed
to establish that boundary. Suppressing all such initialization effects would
require a different loading architecture.
External archive materialization and filesystem preparation are not yet
abortable; do not claim that a pre-aborted caller avoids all pre-attempt setup.
